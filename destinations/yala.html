About Yala National Park

Yala National Park is one of Sri Lanka's best-known protected natural areas. It is located in the southeastern part of the country, across parts of the Southern and Uva provinces. The park is famous for its wildlife, dry-zone landscapes, forests, grasslands, lagoons, and coastal habitats.

Visitors usually explore the park on a guided jeep safari. During a safari, they may see animals in their natural habitats and learn about the importance of protecting Sri Lanka's wildlife and ecosystems. Animal sightings are never guaranteed, so patience and respect for nature are important.

Wildlife of Yala

Yala supports a wide range of wildlife, including Sri Lankan leopards, elephants, sloth bears, deer, crocodiles, monkeys, and many kinds of birds. Its different habitats provide food, shelter, and water for many species.

Leopards

Yala is well known for leopard sightings. These wild cats are shy and elusive, so visitors should observe them quietly and from a safe distance.

Elephants

Asian elephants can be seen moving through grasslands and woodland. Always give elephants plenty of space and never ask a driver to approach them too closely.

Birdlife

The park's wetlands, lagoons, forests, and open areas attract many resident and migratory birds, making Yala interesting for birdwatchers and photographers.

Safari Experience

A jeep safari is the main way to explore the visitor areas of Yala National Park. Early-morning and late-afternoon trips are popular because wildlife may be more active during cooler parts of the day. A knowledgeable driver or guide can help visitors identify animals and understand their behaviour.

Keep in mind that Yala is a natural environment, not a zoo. You may see many animals on one trip and fewer on another. The experience is about appreciating wildlife responsibly, not getting as close as possible to animals.

Visitor Information

Location

Southeastern Sri Lanka, with access commonly arranged through the Tissamaharama area and park entrances such as Palatupana.

Best Time to Visit

Wildlife viewing can vary with rainfall, season, and water availability. Check current park access and conditions before travelling.

What to Bring

Bring drinking water, sun protection, comfortable clothing, binoculars or a camera, and any personal essentials for a safari.

Park access, opening times, entrance fees, and seasonal closures can change. Confirm the latest information with the Department of Wildlife Conservation or an authorised safari operator before your visit.

Travel Tips

  • Follow your guide's instructions and remain inside the safari vehicle unless told otherwise.
  • Keep a safe distance from all wild animals.
  • Do not feed, chase, touch, or make loud noises around wildlife.
  • Never throw rubbish in the park; take everything you bring back with you.
  • Bring water, a hat, and sun protection for the warm weather.
  • Be patient and remember that wildlife sightings cannot be guaranteed.

Plan Your Visit

Use the map to find Yala National Park and plan your route. It can be helpful to stay in or near Tissamaharama before an early safari. Check official visitor guidance before finalising your plans.
